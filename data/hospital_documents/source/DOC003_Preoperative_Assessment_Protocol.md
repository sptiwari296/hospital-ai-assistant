# Pre-operative Assessment Protocol – Elective Surgery

**Document ID:** DOC003 | **Version:** 3.0 | **Effective date:** 2026-06-01 | **Department:** Surgery | **Approved by:** Clinical Guidelines Committee

*Synthetic document created for software development. Not real medical guidance.*

## 1. Purpose
To make sure every patient is medically ready before elective surgery, and to reduce cancellations on the day of surgery.

## 2. Scope
Applies to all elective surgical procedures, including joint replacement (for example total knee replacement).

## 3. Responsibilities
The operating surgeon orders the pre-operative tests. The pre-assessment nurse tracks completion. The anaesthesiologist gives final fitness clearance.

## 4. Required pre-operative tests
### 4.1 Tests for all patients
The following results must be available and recent before surgery:
- Complete Blood Count (CBC) – within 30 days of surgery
- Coagulation profile (PT/INR) – within 30 days of surgery
- 12-lead ECG – within 30 days of surgery
- Chest X-ray (PA view) – within 90 days of surgery

### 4.2 Additional tests for diabetic patients
Patients with diabetes need an HbA1c result within 90 days of surgery. The HbA1c must be **below 8.0 %** for elective surgery to proceed. If it is 8.0 % or above, the patient is referred to Endocrinology and surgery is postponed.

### 4.3 Pre-anaesthesia review
Every patient must complete a pre-anaesthesia review within 30 days before surgery.

## 5. Readiness checklist
Before the day of surgery the following must be confirmed:
1. Surgical consent form signed.
2. Fasting instructions given and acknowledged: no solid food for 8 hours and no clear fluids for 2 hours before surgery.
3. Diabetic patients: the plan for holding metformin and other diabetes medicines is confirmed with the physician.
4. An adult escort is arranged for discharge.

## 6. Insurance pre-authorisation
For insured patients, insurance pre-authorisation must be **approved** before the scheduled date of elective surgery. If pre-authorisation is pending, the surgery date may be moved.

## 7. When surgery is postponed
If any required test is missing, expired or out of range, the surgical team will contact the patient to arrange the test and, if needed, a new surgery date.
