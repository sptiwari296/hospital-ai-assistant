# Discharge Guidelines – Orthopaedic Surgery (Joint Replacement)

**Document ID:** DOC006 | **Version:** 1.3 | **Effective date:** 2026-05-01 | **Department:** Orthopedics | **Approved by:** Head of Orthopaedics

*Synthetic document created for software development. Not real medical guidance.*

## 1. Purpose
To make sure patients leave hospital safely after joint replacement surgery and know how to care for themselves at home.

## 2. Discharge criteria
A patient may be discharged when:
- Pain is controlled with tablets.
- The patient can walk with a walker or crutches and climb a few stairs with help.
- The wound is clean and dry.
- The physiotherapist has approved discharge.

Most patients go home 3 to 5 days after knee replacement surgery.

## 3. Wound care at home
Keep the dressing clean and dry. Do not soak the wound in a bath until the surgeon allows it. Stitches or clips are removed at the follow-up visit, usually 12 to 14 days after surgery.

## 4. Physiotherapy
Continue the home exercise plan every day. Outpatient physiotherapy usually starts within one week of discharge.

## 5. Warning signs – contact the hospital immediately
- Fever above 38 °C
- Increasing redness, swelling or discharge from the wound
- Pain or swelling in the calf, or shortness of breath
- Sudden increase in pain

## 6. Follow-up
A follow-up appointment with the orthopaedic surgeon is booked for about 2 weeks after discharge.

## 7. Before you leave
Collect your discharge summary, prescribed medicines and the final bill. Billing clearance must be completed at the Billing Counter before discharge.
